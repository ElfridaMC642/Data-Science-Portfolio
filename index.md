## Featured Project: Education, Salary, and Working Hours Analysis

### Research Question

How do education levels relate to annual salaries and weekly working hours in the provided dataset?

### Project Overview

This project uses a course dataset of 500 observations containing age, education level, annual salary, and weekly working hours. It uses descriptive data analysis and visualizations to examine how education categories are represented and how salary and working-hour distributions differ across groups.

### Tools and Technical Skills

* **Python and Pandas:** Data loading, grouping, percentages, and filtering.
* **Matplotlib and Squarify:** Treemap visualization.
* **Seaborn:** Violin plots comparing distributions across categories.
* **Data communication:** Interpreting patterns and acknowledging limitations.

### Visualization 1: Education Distribution

!\[Education distribution treemap](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ElfridaMC642/data-science-project/main/education\_treemap.png)

The treemap shows the relative size of each education category. In this dataset, **Bachelor's degree or higher** is the largest group, with 237 out of 500 observations (**47.4%**).

### Visualization 2: Salary and Weekly Working Hours

!\[Salary and working hours violin plots](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ElfridaMC642/data-science-project/main/salary\_hours\_violin\_plots.png)

The two violin plots compare annual salary and weekly working-hour distributions across education levels for individuals **younger than 65**. These plots show differences and variation across groups but do not establish a causal relationship.

### Findings and Limitations

Education groups are represented unevenly in the dataset. The violin plots help visualize differences in the distributions of salary and working hours, but the original sampling method is not documented. The analysis is therefore descriptive and should not be interpreted as proof that education causes particular salaries or work schedules.
